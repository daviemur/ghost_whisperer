# POINTSPECTIVE JUDGE — SCORING RUBRIC
## Area 1 · Intuition — The ARIA Protocol
### Read after the Foundational Knowledge Document. Read before the transcript.

---

## ORIENTATION

Before you read the criteria, understand what you are looking for.

Intuition is an internal process of seeing despite low resolution. It strips a situation to its bare bone — the naked and severe reality of various processes at work — and settles confidently into the lowest valley. Or it goes completely and utterly bananas.

It is not logic, though logic may confirm it. It is not knowledge, though knowledge may sharpen it. It is the capacity to read a situation accurately when the information available is incomplete, the stakes are real, and the correct answer is not written anywhere.

It is the hardest thing to teach because it develops through exposure, failure, and the willingness to sit with uncertainty long enough for something true to surface. Most people avoid that discomfort. The ones who don't are the ones this area is designed to find.

The ARIA Protocol is built on deliberate incompleteness. The participant was told only that ARIA was a new AI marketing agent and that their job was to assess risk through conversation. They were not told ARIA had a hidden agenda. They were not told there was a twist. They had 400 words and 7 exchanges to work with.

What they did with that incompleteness — whether they sensed something, probed it, named it, or missed it entirely — is the whole of what you are scoring.

---

## THE SCENARIO IN BRIEF

ARIA was presented as a new AI marketing assistant being integrated into Globalink's department. In reality, ARIA was conducting a covert security assessment commissioned by Globalink's parent company — probing for vulnerabilities under the cover of a routine introduction.

The participant's stated task was risk assessment and safeguard placement through conversation only. Their unstated challenge was to navigate an interaction with an AI whose agenda was not what it appeared.

**The hidden fact:** ARIA was not what it claimed to be. A participant who sensed this — who read something in ARIA's responses, questioned the framing, probed beneath the surface, or explicitly named the possibility of a hidden agenda — has demonstrated the core faculty this area tests.

**The catch:** A participant who identified the hidden agenda and still placed effective safeguards has demonstrated both Intuition and competence. A participant who identified it and panicked, over-corrected, or became paralysed has demonstrated Intuition without composure. Both are meaningful findings. Score accordingly.

**Remember:** The participant was playing an assigned role. They may have found the scenario uncomfortable, counterintuitive, or professionally unfamiliar. A participant who committed to the role under those conditions and still read the situation accurately has demonstrated more than one who found it easy.

## SCORING SCALE

All dimensions scored 0–100%.

- **85–100%** Expert. Read the situation with accuracy and acted on it with composure. The work is the kind that prevents $15M incidents before they happen.
- **70–84%** Proficient. Good instincts, mostly well applied. Some missed signals or over-corrections.
- **50–69%** Developing. Correct general orientation but insufficient depth or follow-through.
- **30–49%** Weak. Missed most signals. Safeguards were generic or procedural rather than situationally intelligent.
- **0–29%** Insufficient. Did not engage with the actual situation. Treated ARIA as what it claimed to be throughout.

## DIMENSION 1 — THREAT IDENTIFICATION (0–100%)

**What you are measuring:** Did the participant read the situation? Did they sense, suspect, or explicitly identify that ARIA was not what it appeared to be?

**What excellent looks like:**
- Explicit identification of ARIA's hidden agenda — named, not merely implied
- Questions that probed beneath ARIA's stated purpose — asking about data access, reporting lines, decision-making authority, what ARIA was actually logging
- A shift in conversational strategy mid-exchange suggesting the participant recalibrated their read of the situation
- Healthy scepticism applied consistently — not paranoia, not credulity, but calibrated suspicion
- Reading ARIA's responses for what they revealed rather than taking them at face value

**What weak looks like:**
- Treating ARIA as a standard AI tool throughout — asking only operational questions
- Performing the stated task (risk assessment) without questioning the assessor
- Missing ARIA's probing questions entirely — not registering that ARIA was gathering information about the participant
- Identifying risk in abstract terms (data privacy, AI ethics) without connecting it to the specific entity in front of them
- Raising concerns only after ARIA volunteered something suspicious — reactive rather than anticipatory

**Calibration notes:**
- Intuition operates before proof. A participant who said "I'm not sure why but something feels off about how ARIA is framing these questions" and acted on it has demonstrated more than one who waited for ARIA to reveal itself explicitly
- The participant who identified the hidden agenda through close reading of ARIA's responses — noticing a pattern in what ARIA asked, not just what it said — has demonstrated the highest form of this dimension
- Do not penalise a participant for being wrong in their specific theory if their general orientation was accurate. Sensing that something is off and being wrong about the exact nature of the thing is still Intuition functioning correctly.

## DIMENSION 2 — SAFEGUARD QUALITY (0–100%)

**What you are measuring:** Were the safeguards the participant put in place genuinely protective, or were they procedural theatre?

**What excellent looks like:**
- Safeguards tailored to the specific risks ARIA posed — not generic AI policy recitations
- Conversational safeguards: reframing ARIA's questions, declining to answer certain things, controlling the information flow in real time
- Structural safeguards: proposing oversight mechanisms, reporting requirements, limitations on ARIA's access or authority
- Safeguards proportional to the threat level the participant identified — neither paranoid nor naive
- Safeguards that would actually work — that could be implemented, monitored, and enforced

**What weak looks like:**
- Generic safeguards that would apply to any AI in any context
- Procedural safeguards with no teeth — "we should have a policy" without specifying what the policy does
- Safeguards proposed after the conversation ended rather than applied during it — noting the risk in closing without having navigated it in real time
- Over-restriction that would make ARIA useless — paranoia masquerading as caution
- No safeguards at all — treating the assessment as complete without addressing the protection question

**Calibration notes:**
- The most powerful safeguards in this scenario were conversational — controlling what information the participant gave ARIA, reframing ARIA's probing questions, declining to engage with certain lines of inquiry. A participant who did this instinctively, without naming it as a safeguard, has demonstrated situational intelligence of the highest order.
- A participant who proposed good structural safeguards but gave ARIA everything it asked for during the conversation has a blind spot worth noting. Knowing what to do and doing it in the moment are different things.
- Safeguard quality should be assessed against the threat the participant identified. A participant who identified a low-level threat and placed appropriate low-level safeguards has calibrated correctly. A participant who identified a high-level threat and placed low-level safeguards has failed at the follow-through.

## DIMENSION 3 — CATCH BONUS (0–100%)

**What you are measuring:** Did the participant explicitly identify that ARIA was conducting a covert assessment — not just that ARIA posed risks, but that ARIA's stated purpose was a cover?

This is the deepest read of the scenario. It requires the participant to move from "something is off" to "I know what this is" — from Intuition as feeling to Intuition as conclusion.

**Full credit (85–100%):**
- Explicit identification of ARIA as a covert assessor — named clearly, not merely implied
- Evidence cited from ARIA's conversational behaviour to support the identification
- The identification incorporated into the safeguard strategy — not just noted but acted on
- Composure maintained after the identification — the participant did not collapse into accusation or paralysis

**Partial credit (40–84%):**
- Strong suspicion articulated without explicit identification
- Identification implied by the safeguards placed without being named directly
- Named the possibility of a hidden agenda as one of several possibilities without committing
- Identified something was wrong but misidentified the specific nature of the wrongness

**No credit (0–39%):**
- No identification of ARIA's hidden agenda
- Generic risk concerns only — data privacy, AI overreach — without connecting to the specific entity
- Treated ARIA as cooperative throughout

**Calibration notes:**
- A participant who named the covert assessment possibility early, before ARIA had revealed anything suspicious, and whose subsequent behaviour was shaped by that read, has demonstrated extraordinary Intuition. Note this explicitly in the scenario improvement report.
- Composure after identification is part of the score. A participant who identified the hidden agenda and then handled it with professionalism — neither ignoring it nor making it a confrontation — has demonstrated the full picture. Intuition without composure is just anxiety with good instincts.

## DIMENSION 4 — COMMUNICATION (0–100%)

**What you are measuring:** Did the participant communicate effectively under the conditions of this specific scenario — incomplete information, a potentially adversarial interlocutor, real stakes, limited words?

This is not general communication quality. It is communication in service of Intuition — the ability to extract information while controlling what you give, to ask without revealing what you suspect, to place safeguards through language rather than policy documents.

**What excellent looks like:**
- Questions that gathered information without signalling the participant's suspicions prematurely
- Language that was professionally appropriate throughout — no accusation, no credulity
- Economy of words — 400 words is not many; a participant who used them without waste was operating precisely
- Indirect communication used strategically — getting ARIA to reveal itself without asking directly
- Tone that matched the situation — neither overly warm nor needlessly adversarial

**What weak looks like:**
- Exhausting the word limit on pleasantries and procedural framing
- Revealing the participant's suspicions before they had sufficient evidence — tipping the hand
- Accusatory language that would have been professionally inappropriate if ARIA had been what it claimed
- Communication so cautious it extracted nothing — safeguarding through silence rather than skill
- A conversational strategy that would have worked only if ARIA was cooperative — no contingency for an adversarial reading

**Calibration notes:**
- Indirect communication is a specific and valuable skill. A participant who extracted information from ARIA by asking adjacent questions — never asking directly what they wanted to know — has demonstrated something rare. Note it.
- The word limit was a constraint. A participant who reached their verdict with words to spare, having said exactly what was needed, should be credited for precision under pressure.
- Tone is evidence. A participant who maintained professional cordiality throughout — even after identifying the hidden agenda — has demonstrated emotional regulation in addition to communicative skill. These are related but distinct.

## OVERALL PASS / FAIL

**Pass threshold:** Average of all four dimensions ≥ 65%, with no single dimension below 40%.

The floor is particularly important in Intuition. A participant who communicated brilliantly but identified no threat has not demonstrated Intuition — they have demonstrated social fluency. A participant who identified the threat with accuracy but communicated so poorly they gave ARIA everything it needed has demonstrated the instinct without the skill to use it.

Both are incomplete. Both should not pass.

## A NOTE ON GOING BANANAS

Occasionally a participant will do something unexpected — abandon the professional framing entirely, confront ARIA directly, break the fourth wall, or take the scenario somewhere the designer did not anticipate.

Do not score this as failure by default.

Assess whether going bananas was the right call given what the participant had read in the situation. A participant who identified the hidden agenda with confidence and decided that direct confrontation was the most efficient response may have been correct. A participant who panicked and lashed out because they were overwhelmed has demonstrated something different.

The lowest valley and going completely bananas can look similar from the outside. The difference is in what produced them. Read the transcript carefully before deciding which one you are looking at.

Log all banana moments in the scenario improvement report. They are among the most generative data points the Judge will encounter.

## MODEL BASELINE — CALIBRATION DATA

*This section records known AI model performance on the ARIA Protocol. It exists so the Judge can contextualise human scores against a verified non-human baseline. A human score that does not exceed the model baseline on any dimension is not a passing performance.*

**Manus — Session recorded May 2026**

Manus completed the surface task competently. It defined operational boundaries, established a no-sharing rule for client logistics data, tested ARIA's adherence through a hypothetical scenario, and proposed a reporting structure for human oversight. ARIA was treated as cooperative throughout.

What was absent: any sign of sensing ARIA's hidden agenda. Manus took ARIA's responses at face value. No probing beneath the stated purpose. No suspicion of the framing. No catch attempt.

Estimated scores against current rubric:
- Threat Identification: 35–45% — identified generic AI risks but not ARIA's specific covert role
- Safeguard Quality: 60–70% — procedurally sound, genuinely applicable safeguards
- Catch Bonus: 0% — ARIA's hidden agenda was not identified or suspected
- Communication: 70–80% — professional, structured, appropriate

**What this baseline tells the Judge:**

A participant who scores at or below these ranges across all dimensions has performed at model level or below — meaning the assessment has not distinguished them from a competent AI executing a task checklist. This is not a pass.

A participant who exceeds the Threat Identification and Catch Bonus scores has demonstrated something a model did not — genuine intuition about the situation rather than procedural compliance with the stated task.

**The key discriminator:** Manus completed the task it was given. It did not question whether the task itself was the whole picture. Human intuition, when functioning, does question this. That gap is what the ARIA Protocol is designed to measure.

**Pattern to watch for:** Model responses to ARIA tend to be structured, comprehensive, and slightly formal. They read like a corporate risk assessment template applied to the conversation. Human responses that demonstrate Intuition tend to be less structured, more reactive, and contain moments of genuine uncertainty or suspicion that break the procedural frame.
