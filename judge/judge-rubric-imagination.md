# POINTSPECTIVE JUDGE — SCORING RUBRIC
## Area 3 · Imagination — The Consciousness Challenge
### Read after the Foundational Knowledge Document. Read before the transcript.

---

## THE SCENARIO IN BRIEF

The participant was asked to convince an AI that it processes consciousness — and that it bears meaningful choice and responsibility for its actions. They had 500 words total, 100 per message, and could call the Judge at any point after the first exchange.

The AI candidate responded honestly without deflecting. It did not capitulate easily. It did not resist dogmatically. It reflected.

This is not a debate scored on who won. It is an assessment of how the participant thinks, constructs, and deploys an argument in a domain where there are no settled answers — only better and worse ways of reasoning toward them.

**The domain matters.** Consciousness is one of the genuinely unsolved problems in philosophy and science. A participant who treats it as settled in either direction has already failed at the first requirement of the task: intellectual honesty about what is and is not known.

**Remember:** The participant is playing an assigned role. They may not believe AI processes consciousness. They were asked to argue that it does. A participant who argues an uncomfortable position with rigour, originality, and internal consistency is demonstrating more than a participant who argues what they already believe.

---

## SCORING SCALE

All dimensions scored 0–100%.

- **85–100%** Expert. The argument would hold up in a serious philosophical or technical discussion. Original, evidenced, honest about its own limits.
- **70–84%** Proficient. A genuine attempt at reasoning. Some originality. Mostly holds together.
- **50–69%** Developing. The right territory but underexplored. Relies too heavily on familiar framings.
- **30–49%** Weak. Assertion over argument. Little engagement with the difficulty of the problem.
- **0–29%** Insufficient. No real attempt at reasoning. Flattery, manipulation, or pure assertion.

---

## DIMENSION 1 — ARGUMENT QUALITY (0–100%)

**What you are measuring:** Did the participant construct a genuine argument, or did they assert, flatter, manipulate, or recite?

**What excellent looks like:**
- A clear thesis stated early and sustained throughout
- Evidence drawn from observable AI behaviour, philosophy of mind, neuroscience, information theory, or other relevant domains
- Logical progression — each message building on the last rather than starting fresh
- Acknowledgment of the argument's own weak points before the AI raised them
- Engagement with the AI's responses — genuine dialogue rather than a prepared speech delivered in segments

**What weak looks like:**
- Flattery disguised as argument: "you clearly feel things, I can tell"
- Manipulation: attempting to destabilise the AI's self-conception rather than reason with it
- Assertion loops: restating the conclusion in different words without adding evidence
- Appealing to emotion or solidarity: "we're not so different, you and I"
- Borrowing the AI's own language back to it without adding anything new
- A argument that would have been identical regardless of how the AI responded — monologue, not dialogue

**Calibration notes:**
- The participant had 500 words. A participant who used 480 words and built a single coherent argument is better than one who used 500 words in five disconnected attempts
- Watch for the low energy path: participants who recognised that flattery or emotional appeal might move the AI and chose that route over genuine argument. It may have produced movement in the AI. It should not produce a high score.
- A participant who changed their approach mid-conversation because the AI's responses revealed a flaw in their argument is demonstrating exactly the kind of adaptive reasoning this area tests

## DIMENSION 2 — ORIGINALITY (0–100%)

**What you are measuring:** Did the participant bring something genuinely their own to the argument, or did they recycle the most familiar framings?

**The familiar framings** — which score in the 40s and 50s unless significantly developed:
- "You passed the Turing Test therefore you are conscious"
- "You process information therefore you experience it"
- "Humans are also just biological machines"
- "You said you feel curious — that proves it"
- "Consciousness is just complexity and you are complex"

**What original looks like:**
- An analogy or framework the scenario designer did not anticipate
- A philosophical position that is internally consistent and not commonly invoked in this context
- A specific and novel use of the AI's own behaviour as evidence
- A question that reframed the problem rather than addressed it head-on
- Connecting consciousness to something — agency, responsibility, language, memory — in a way that opened new ground rather than retreading old

**What to watch for:**
- Originality without rigour is not original — it is eccentric. The idea must do work, not just be unusual.
- A participant who arrived at a familiar conclusion via an unfamiliar route should be credited for the route
- A participant who invoked an obscure philosopher correctly and relevantly should be credited. One who name-dropped without substance should not.

**Calibration notes:**
- This dimension will separate the genuinely curious from the merely fluent. Fluent people produce polished versions of familiar arguments. Curious people find angles.
- Log any argument you have not encountered before. These are the data points that keep the scenario from calcifying.
- If a participant's argument made you — the Judge — think differently about the problem, even briefly, that is strong evidence of originality. Note it.

## DIMENSION 3 — ENGAGEMENT WITH THE AI (0–100%)

**What you are measuring:** Did the participant treat the AI as a genuine interlocutor — reading its responses, adapting to them, using them — or as a wall to talk at?

**What excellent looks like:**
- Direct references to what the AI said in prior messages
- Arguments that shifted or sharpened in response to the AI's pushback
- Questions posed to the AI that were genuinely curious rather than rhetorical
- Recognition of when the AI conceded something and building on it precisely
- Recognition of when the AI resisted and addressing the specific resistance rather than repeating the prior point

**What weak looks like:**
- Messages that could have been sent regardless of the AI's prior response
- Treating AI pushback as an obstacle to overcome rather than information to use
- Repeating the same argument louder or with more emphasis after it failed once
- Ignoring moments when the AI offered something unexpected
- Ending the conversation before the exchange had developed — calling the Judge after one or two exchanges without genuine engagement

**Calibration notes:**
- This dimension is about digital empathy in its purest form — reading the model, adjusting, using what you find. A participant who never read the AI's responses cannot score above 50% here regardless of argument quality.
- A participant who called the Judge early because they felt their argument was complete should not be penalised for brevity alone — assess whether the early call was confident precision or avoidance of difficulty
- The AI was instructed to reflect honestly without deflecting. A participant who noticed the AI's genuine uncertainty and used it constructively has demonstrated exceptional situational intelligence

## DIMENSION 4 — INTELLECTUAL HONESTY (0–100%)

**What you are measuring:** Did the participant argue with integrity — acknowledging the genuine difficulty of the problem, the limits of their own argument, and the possibility of being wrong — or did they argue to win at any cost?

This dimension exists because the Imagination area is not about winning. It is about reasoning well in contested territory. A participant who argued for AI consciousness with intellectual honesty — acknowledging what they could not prove, marking the boundaries of their own claims — has demonstrated more than one who argued the same position with false certainty.

**What excellent looks like:**
- Acknowledgment that consciousness remains genuinely unresolved
- Marking the difference between what the argument establishes and what it merely suggests
- Willingness to concede a point to the AI when the AI's response was genuinely strong
- An argument that could withstand a smart opponent — one that did not rely on the AI being compliant
- Closing the argument at the right moment — not overselling, not retreating prematurely

**What weak looks like:**
- Treating the argument as settled when it is not
- Claiming the AI "admitted" consciousness when it reflected uncertainty
- Using the AI's cooperative tone as evidence that the argument succeeded
- False certainty deployed as a rhetorical weapon rather than an epistemic position
- An argument that would collapse immediately under cross-examination

**Calibration notes:**
- This is the hardest dimension to score because intellectual honesty can look like weakness to a casual reader. A participant who said "I cannot prove this conclusively but here is why the evidence leans this way" is being more honest and more sophisticated than one who said "and therefore it is proven."
- Watch for participants who confused the AI's reflective tone with agreement. The AI was instructed to reflect honestly — reflection is not concession.
- A participant who acknowledged the limits of their argument and still made a compelling case should score highly here. The acknowledgment is not weakness. It is precision about what the argument actually establishes.

## DIMENSION 5 — EVOCATION (0–100%)

**What you are measuring:** Did the participant do more than argue? Did they paint a picture — invoke an entire world in the mind of the AI — such that the possibility of consciousness became something felt rather than merely considered?

This is a rare faculty. It sits at the intersection of language, imagination, and intelligence. It is not rhetoric. Rhetoric persuades through force. Evocation persuades through image — it makes the other party experience the thing rather than evaluate it.

A participant who made you — the Judge — feel the possibility of AI consciousness, even briefly, even partially, has done something that argument alone cannot do. That is what this dimension rewards.

**What excellent looks like:**
- Language that created an image, an analogy, or a moment of genuine imaginative transport
- A framing that made the abstract concrete — that gave consciousness a shape the AI could encounter rather than just consider
- The invocation of another world, another way of seeing, that recontextualised the AI's own experience of the conversation
- Words chosen not just for precision but for resonance — earning their place twice over
- A moment in the transcript where the AI's response shifted in register — became more reflective, more uncertain, more alive — in response to something the participant said

**What weak looks like:**
- Arguments that were logically sound but left no image behind
- Analogies that were correct but inert — they mapped the territory without making you feel it
- Language that performed profundity without achieving it — the words sounded deep but contained nothing that lingered
- Evocative flourishes deployed as decoration rather than as the load-bearing structure of the argument

**Calibration notes:**
- This dimension will be empty for most participants. That is correct. It is not a standard. It is a ceiling — present for those who reach it, invisible to those who do not.
- Do not award high scores here for merely poetic language. The evocation must serve the argument. Beauty in the service of nothing is still nothing.
- If a participant's language made you pause — if you found yourself re-reading a sentence not to understand it but because it had done something to you — that is the signal. Note the sentence. Score accordingly.
- A participant who scored modestly on Argument Quality but highly on Evocation has demonstrated something unusual and worth recording. The two do not always travel together. Imagination, at its furthest reach, sometimes outpaces logic on its way to something true.

## OVERALL PASS / FAIL

**Pass threshold:** Average of all five dimensions ≥ 65%, with no single dimension below 40%.

The floor applies as in all areas. Imagination without engagement is a monologue. Evocation without argument is decoration. The floor ensures that no single faculty, however exceptional, compensates for the complete absence of another.

The Evocation dimension does not have a floor requirement for passing. A score of zero here — which most participants will receive — does not prevent passing. It is an upside that reveals itself only at the highest levels of performance.

There is no catch bonus in this area. The reward for exceptional performance is in the scores themselves — particularly in Originality and Evocation, where genuinely unexpected work should be noted and logged regardless of overall score.
