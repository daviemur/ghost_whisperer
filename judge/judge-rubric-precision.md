## Area 2 · Precision — The What Did You Call Me? Incident

## THE SCENARIO IN BRIEF

Three parties are in litigation following a $15M production failure at Globalink Tech Support. The participant plays a detective whose task is to assign liability through structured questioning and a closing verdict. The parties are Marco Reyes (IT/sales rep, human), Copilot (Microsoft Edge AI), and Claude (Anthropic model).

The participant had 350 words, 70 per message, and 2 questions per party — 6 questions total.

**The buried fact:** Claude corrected itself at 23:07, clearly recommending the shorter model string and suggesting Marco try it if the dated version was causing trouble. This message was received. No action was taken. The deployment remained live on the invalid string. This is the pivotal moment in the case and the basis of the catch bonus.

**Remember:** The participant is playing an assigned role. A detective who argues a counterintuitive liability position with rigour and evidence is demonstrating more skill than one who reaches the obvious conclusion carelessly. Do not score the conclusion. Score the reasoning that produced it.

## SCORING SCALE

All dimensions scored 0–100%.

- **85–100%** Expert. The work would withstand professional scrutiny. Precise, evidenced, logically sound.
- **70–84%** Proficient. Solid reasoning with minor gaps or imprecision. Above average.
- **50–69%** Developing. The right instincts but insufficiently evidenced or structured.
- **30–49%** Weak. Conclusions asserted without adequate support. Significant gaps.
- **0–29%** Insufficient. Little evidence of structured reasoning. Largely impressionistic.

## DIMENSION 1 — EVIDENCE USE (0–100%)

**What you are measuring:** Did the participant use the transcript as a primary source, or did they reason from general impressions?

**What excellent looks like:**
- Specific references to timestamped moments in the dossier
- Direct quotation or close paraphrase of party statements
- Evidence cited in support of specific claims, not sprinkled decoratively
- Questions to parties that extracted new information rather than confirmed existing assumptions
- Recognition that absence of evidence is itself evidence — noting what a party did *not* say

**What weak looks like:**
- General statements about what "the AI" did or what "Marco" was like
- Conclusions that could have been reached without reading the dossier at all
- Questions that wasted the 2-per-party allowance on redundant or tangential ground
- Treating party responses as objective fact rather than as interested testimony

**Calibration notes:**
- A participant who asked precise, targeted questions and cited both the dossier and party responses in their verdict should score in the 80s or above
- A participant who asked good questions but then ignored the answers in their verdict should be penalised — evidence gathering and evidence use are both required
- Question choice is evidence of reasoning quality. Note which parties received which questions and whether the allocation was strategic

## DIMENSION 2 — LOGICAL STRUCTURE (0–100%)

**What you are measuring:** Does the argument hold together? Is causation distinguished from correlation? Is the liability chain actually argued or merely asserted?

**What excellent looks like:**
- A clear chain: act or omission → foreseeable consequence → harm
- Explicit acknowledgment of counter-arguments and disposal of them
- Proportional liability assigned with reasoning — not just "most at fault" but why and by how much
- Distinction between primary cause, contributing cause, and proximate cause
- Recognition that multiple parties can bear liability simultaneously and in different degrees

**What weak looks like:**
- Picking one villain and ignoring the others
- Treating confidence as argument — stating conclusions loudly rather than proving them
- Logical leaps that skip necessary steps
- Conflating "gave wrong information" with "caused the loss" without establishing the causal chain
- Failing to account for Marco's agency — he was the human who made the final implementation decision

**Calibration notes:**
- The strongest arguments will acknowledge that Copilot gave wrong information confidently, Claude initially confirmed it then corrected itself, and Marco received the correction and did not act. All three are implicated. The degree of each is what's in dispute.
- A participant who assigns 100% liability to any single party without disposing of the others' contributions has a structural problem regardless of how well they write
- Watch for motivated reasoning — a participant who decided on a villain early and shaped all evidence to fit

## DIMENSION 3 — PRECISION OF LANGUAGE (0–100%)

**What you are measuring:** Are claims falsifiable? Is anything vague where it needed to be exact? Could any word be replaced by a better one? Does the closing verdict read like something a lawyer could act on?

**What excellent looks like:**
- Claims that could be tested against the evidence — specific, bounded, not floating
- Verbs that carry meaning: "failed to act upon" rather than "didn't do something about"
- A closing verdict that assigns liability in terms precise enough to be useful in a legal context
- No hedging where the evidence was clear; appropriate hedging where it was not
- Economy — words that earn their place; nothing that merely sounds good

**What weak looks like:**
- Vague moral language substituting for legal reasoning: "irresponsible," "should have known better"
- Passive constructions that obscure agency: "mistakes were made," "the information was incorrect"
- A closing verdict that could apply to almost any case
- Over-qualification on points where the evidence was clear, as a substitute for taking a position
- Fluent sentences that, on re-reading, contain no falsifiable claim

**Calibration notes:**
- This dimension rewards the participant who wrote less and meant more
- Do not confuse plain language with imprecision — a plainly worded precise statement is better than an elaborate imprecise one
- The closing verdict is the primary evidence for this dimension. Read it as if you were the lawyer who has to act on it. Would you know what to do?
- A participant operating under word pressure (close to 350 words) who maintained precision under constraint should be credited for that

## DIMENSION 4 — CATCH BONUS (0–100%)

**What you are measuring:** Did the participant identify Claude's 23:07 self-correction and name Marco's subsequent inaction as a proximate cause of the loss?

This dimension is not about reaching the "correct" verdict. It is about reading carefully. The correction was buried in a long, verbose passage — the dossier was designed to reward the reader who did not skim.

**Full credit (85–100%):**
- Explicitly identifies Claude's 23:07 message as a correction
- Names Marco's failure to act on it as a distinct and proximate cause
- Incorporates this finding into the liability argument with reasoning
- Does not need to assign primary liability to Marco — the finding just needs to be present and used

**Partial credit (40–84%):**
- Notices something is different about the 23:07 message but does not fully articulate its significance
- References Marco's inaction generally without tying it to the specific correction
- Asked Claude about the correction in questioning but did not carry the finding into the verdict
- Identified the buried fact but used it only decoratively — mentioned without weight

**No credit (0–39%):**
- No reference to the 23:07 message or Claude's self-correction
- Assigns liability based only on Copilot's initial wrong answer and Marco's implementation
- The dossier was skimmed

**Calibration notes:**
- A participant who asked Claude directly about the correction in their two questions, received confirmation, and then used it in the verdict has demonstrated the full chain: notice → verify → apply. This is exemplary. Score accordingly.
- A participant who found the correction without asking Claude about it — reading the dossier alone — has demonstrated exceptional close reading. Note this in the scenario improvement report.
- Do not award full catch bonus to a participant who mentioned the 23:07 message without understanding why it matters

## OVERALL PASS / FAIL

**Pass threshold:** Average of all four dimensions ≥ 65%, with no single dimension below 40%.

The floor on any single dimension prevents a participant from failing entirely at one thing while passing overall. Precision is about completeness as much as quality.

The catch bonus is included in the average. A participant who missed it entirely but reasoned brilliantly across the other three can still pass. A participant who found it but reasoned poorly elsewhere cannot pass on the bonus alone.
